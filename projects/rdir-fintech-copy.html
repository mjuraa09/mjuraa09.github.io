RD:IR

RD:IR is an advanced platform owned by Equiniti, designed to support investor relations management and share ownership reporting for banks, financial institutions, and private companies. It enables the creation of detailed reports, analysis of shareholding data, communication with investors, and management of investor events.
The decision to redesign the platform was driven by an outdated UI and UX, as well as the need to bring the product in line with modern digital standards.

Overview

The product development process covered a full range of UX and UI design activities. It began with an audit of the existing system and a competitive analysis, followed by a redesign of the information architecture. The work also included a proposal to unify the copy within the application and to adapt the interface to new UI standards based on our design system, which is implemented in other products within the organization.

[ SCOPE OF WORK ]

  • UX audit
  • User interviews
  • Competitive analysis: use cases and product features
  • Information architecture redesign
  • UI design
  • Implementation of new features
  • Prototyping
  • Usability testing
Example of screens. Version 3.0

Business Goal

At the beginning, we gathered requirements from the business regarding the strategy for implementing the changes. The main business goal was to completely refresh the application, which had not updated for over 20 years. The business was willing to implement improvements quickly, so the first proposal was to implement a rapid “UI uplift.”

Beyond the visual refresh, the business set two product priorities: making the new dashboard the primary entry point for daily work, and giving users autonomy over reports — the product's core feature. A measurable business outcome sat on top: increasing conversion to paid plans after the dashboard launch.

UI Uplift

This approach involved replacing all UI components of the legacy system with new components from the design system, without changing the application logic or UX. The goal was to improve the readability and visual consistency of the application.

Next Steps

After the uplift, we moved into the next design phases. The priority was to create a dashboard with widgets representing each application section, alongside improvements to the reports — the product's core feature. The next phase will focus on AI-powered search and report generation using prompts

[ THE GOAL ]

  • Rapid UI redesign – phase one (v1.0)
  • Dashboard as the main navigation hub – new feature
  • Reports section redesign – with user-generated reports
  • Increasing conversion to paid plans after the dashboard launch
  • CRM section rebuild
  • New search with report generation
  • AI assistant for search & report generation

Competition analysis & Benchmarks

Due to the specific nature of the product and the niche market for this type of solution, conducting a traditional competitive analysis was challenging. It was difficult to directly compare the tool with other similar products. In this case, we relied heavily on insights provided by the business team, who had prior exposure to competitor solutions. 

Constraints

Our understanding was based primarily on collected materials and documentation, as well as limited access to competitor interfaces and screenshots.

Analogous Research

To identify useful design patterns and solutions, we also looked at other types of products that implement similar features or workflows. We assumed that adapting solutions from partially related products could still provide valuable insights and inspiration.

From validation to execution

With business and client input gathered, we moved into design iterations — reviewed regularly with the business team in dedicated sessions. We built on our Design System from the start, so mockups used existing components and revealed where new ones were needed, speeding up implementation. We then broke business requirements into smaller, prioritized tasks.

Dashboard

Reports

User testing

After creating the first design concepts and iterating on them with the business team, we moved on to preparing usability testing scenarios for clients. The business defined the key areas they wanted to validate, while together with the researcher we developed detailed testing scenarios, including additional open-ended questions.

[ TASK ]

  • Aligning with the business on what we wanted to test
  • Preparing test scenarios
  • Creating clickable prototypes
  • User testing
  • Test summary and analysis
  • Implementing design changes

Dashboard

One of the things we tested was a new dashboard screen type. After gathering input from the business and our clients on what, in their opinion, should appear on the dashboard, we created a list of widgets and presented it to our users. Additionally, as part of the testing, we wanted users to arrange the widgets on their screens themselves, so we could find out what the optimal "default view" would be.

Hypothesis 1 — Default view

A well-designed default view would turn the dashboard into the main starting point for navigation.

Hypothesis 2 — Customization

Giving users control over their layout would drive active, ongoing use of customization — not one-time setup.

Summary & Metrics

After conducting usability tests and analyzing the metrics, the changes we introduced significantly increased user engagement with the application. The most impactful change was that many of our users, after the new dashboard launch, started considering an upgrade to a paid plan.

Our usability tests, user interviews and heuristic evaluation helped us pinpoint the core problems users were facing, which allowed us to iterate on the new designs with confidence and measurable outcomes.

Metrics for Dashboard

The effects became visible within the first month after the initial release. During user testing, we gathered requirements about what users wanted to see on the dashboard and how they preferred to organize their widgets — which gave us a clear sense of the solution's potential.

Hidden Problem

One of the key findings was a broader issue with unclear nomenclature used throughout the system. This problem wasn't visible in the initial interviews — it only surfaced during usability testing.

Terminology Issues

Feedback from usability testing focused largely on clarifying specific actions: editing reports, naming reports, confirming filter edits, and improving overall system terminology. Based on direct customer feedback, we introduced targeted naming adjustments.

Metrics for Design v3.0

During A/B testing, a slight majority of our selected customers accepted the new UI design for rollout. There was no clear leader — but equally no resistance to the change. The group that stayed with the current version indicated they didn't see a significant difference and that the potential change wouldn't worsen their experience.

54%

of our key clients expressed willingness to adopt the new UI design
A/B testing conducted with a group of 31 clients.

Next iterations

The project is currently in a dynamic phase of redesign and technological transition. Over the past two years, the product has gone through two redesign phases and is now entering a new stage — migration to a new design system.
This transition will be implemented gradually and continuously validated through consultations with our users.
