mFlota – Orlen

This is an application for medium and large companies where users can purchase fuel, pay highway tickets, or car washes without using cash or a credit card. Transactions can be completed using the app.

Overview

Orlen had an old app for mobile transactions for companies. They decided to refresh it and design a new app for their product. Additionally, Orlen wanted to add a new feature - the purchase of highway tickets.  The core feature of this app is purchasing via QR code scanning, so we focused on designing this process to be simple and easy for users.

[ SCOPE OF WORK ]

  • UX audit
  • Competition analysis: use cases, product features
  • The new information architecture
  • The new UI design
  • Implementation new features
  • Prototyping
  • Usability testing
Scope of work

The product goal

Our goal was to deliver a new product solution as quickly as possible. The main feature of the application was to scan a QR code from the fuel dispenser and pay for the transaction.

One Cart, More Items

An additional function was to create a shopping cart with the option to purchase other items available at the station, such as windshield washer fluid or a snack bar, and pay for everything using the same method without having to wait in line at the checkout.

The Merge Challenge

The challenge we faced was: how to combine the fuel cart with the retail cart and complete the payment in a single process?

[ THE GOAL ]

  • New transaction process: payment via QR code scanning
  • Creation of a shared payment cart
  • Creation of a payment option for highways and car washes at stations
  • Ability to add a card to the app and generate spending statistics
Section: My Card

To check the old app

Before initiating any workshop process, we needed to understand how the existing app operates. We conducted an audit of the app to determine how its features work, to assess the app's performance, to understand the purchasing process, and to measure the time a user takes to complete the purchase process. We also investigated any additional facilities for users, such as the option to add something to their favorites as a shortcut.
UX audit

To understand the user flow

The next meeting was specifically aimed at understanding the flow of the app: why it works as it does and what we might change. The functioning of Oreln’s app was dependent on the back-end perspective, which is why some of the application's feedback for users could be confusing. After this lengthy workshop session, we decided to slightly redesign the flow and agree on some changes with the clients.

Summary of usability tests

After the testing process, we received feedback highlighting some minor and significant issues. I have since made the necessary adjustments to the project.

Postponed for Now

The flow for "Highway Services" proved challenging for users, leading us to recommend its exclusion from the current development process with plans to enhance it in the near future.

Inherited Flow

This service was sourced from another provider, and although we adopted their flow, we did introduce a different UI design. We were not in a position to make any alterations to the original flow.

A Gap in Research

It's worth noting that the original provider had not conducted user testing for the "Highway Services" feature.

Metrics & Development

During our collaboration with the client, we couldn't verify data from the legacy app — the client wasn't tracking user behavior, time-on-task, or drop-off points.What we could measure was our own data, gathered throughout the project: insights from interviews, usability tests, and stakeholder workshops. We then compared those findings against the outcomes of our work — whether the changes we introduced addressed both client requirements and user needs.
